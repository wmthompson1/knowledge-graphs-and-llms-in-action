In manufacturing and supply chain management, "turn" usually refers to Inventory Turns (or inventory turnover), which represents the number of times a company sells and replaces its stock of inventory over a specific period (typically a year). [1] 
When evaluated specifically in the context of customer orders, "turn" measures the velocity and efficiency with which customer demand pulls material through the production lifecycle.
------------------------------
## How "Turns" Relate to Customer Orders## 1. The Pull System Velocity
In modern lean manufacturing—especially in Make-to-Order (MTO) or Assemble-to-Order (ATO) environments—customer orders act as the "pull" mechanism. A higher number of turns means that incoming customer orders are rapidly pulling raw materials, converting them into finished goods, and shipping them out the door. [1, 2, 3, 4] 
## 2. The Formula Behind the Metric
Financially, inventory turns are calculated as:
$$\text{Inventory Turns} = \frac{\text{Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)}}{\text{Average Value of Inventory}}$$ 

* Cost of Goods Sold (COGS): This represents the direct manufacturing costs of the products successfully delivered to fill customer orders.
* Average Value of Inventory: The capital tied up in raw materials, work-in-progress (WIP), and finished goods waiting for an order. [5, 6] 

A manufacturing facility fulfilling a high volume of customer orders without letting stock sit around will achieve high "turns". [1] 
## 3. Different Types of Turns in Production
Depending on where the customer order injects itself into the manufacturing process, a planner might look at different turn metrics: [6] 

* Finished Goods Turnover: How quickly a completed product is shipped out once a customer order clears.
* WIP (Work-in-Process) Turnover: How quickly components move from one manufacturing stage to the next to satisfy the order schedule. [6] 

------------------------------
## Directly Related Metrics
If you hear the word "turn" used alongside customer orders, it is often discussed alongside these closely related time-based metrics:

| Metric | Definition | Relationship to "Turns" |
|---|---|---|
| Days Sales of Inventory (DSI) | The average number of days it takes to turn inventory into completed sales. | It is the inverse of turns (e.g., 6 turns a year ≈ 60 days on hand). |
| Order Lead Time | The total time from when a customer places an order to when they receive it. | Shorter lead times typically correspond to faster manufacturing velocity and higher turns. |

## Summary
In short, a "turn" is the ultimate health check of how fast a factory converts its physical assets into fulfilled customer orders and revenue. High turns mean high efficiency; low turns mean cash is trapped on the warehouse shelves. [1, 7, 8, 9]
